Before you set out

Four things worth knowing that no tourist listing will tell you.

There is no Kierkegaard museum in Copenhagen

Denmark's richest collection of his belongings — the writing desk with its ink stain, his pipes and keys, a lock of his hair, and the engagement ring he gave Regine Olsen and had resized to wear himself after she returned it — belongs to Københavns Museum but is not on permanent display since the museum moved to Stormgade in 2020. A visitor centre, Søren Kierkegaards Verden, was approved in outline in April 2025 for the old University Library in Fiolstræde, but funding was deferred again in the 2026 city budget to the Budget 2027 negotiations. Check before making a special trip.

Three things to get right on the ground

  • Don't go to Vor Frue Kirke for the baptism. It's the natural assumption — Vor Frue was the family's parish church and is where his funeral was held — but it stood a bombardment ruin until 1829. He was baptised at Helligåndskirken on 3 June 1813 and confirmed at Trinitatis Kirke in April 1828.
  • "Nytorv 2" is the right historic address and the wrong thing to type. It no longer exists in the Danish register, and geocoders resolve it to Kongens Nytorv 2, most of a kilometre away. Navigate to Frederiksberggade 1, where the plaque is.
  • Frederiks Hospital, not Frederiksberg. He died on Bredgade, in what is now Designmuseum Danmark. Frederiksberg Hospital is a different institution on the other side of the city — one letter apart, 4 km apart on the ground.

The walking route is the point

Kierkegaard composed on foot — "I have walked myself into my best thoughts" — and took daily "people-baths" along Strøget, buttonholing acquaintances. That is why the caricatures in Corsaren from January 1846 wounded him so precisely: they were visual, so Copenhageners who had never read a line could now recognise him in the street. Schoolboys shouted "Enten–Eller!" after him. He gave up the sociable street life, took carriages into the countryside instead, and turned to the signed religious writings. The 21-stop route on the map is roughly 6 km end to end and ends at his grave.

Coordinate provenance

Addresses were resolved against DAWA, the official Danish national address register, and monuments and the grave against tagged OpenStreetMap nodes cross-checked with Wikidata. Two locations are marked as estimates in their detail panel: the cast-metal pavement quotation on Nytorv, whose exact placement in the square is not published, and the Rækker Mølle memorial room, geocoded to building level. Everything else is surveyed to five decimal places.
